What is the Nakamoto Coefficient?

The Nakamoto Coefficient is one of the most widely used metrics for measuring blockchain decentralization. It represents the minimum number of independent validators or miners that would need to collude to compromise a blockchain network. A higher Nakamoto Coefficient indicates stronger decentralization, greater censorship resistance, and improved resilience against coordinated attacks.

For Proof-of-Stake (PoS) blockchains, the Nakamoto Coefficient measures how many validators collectively control at least 33% of the total stake or voting power. For Proof-of-Work (PoW) blockchains such as Bitcoin, it measures how many miners control more than 50% of the network's total hash power. This makes the Nakamoto Coefficient one of the most practical ways to compare decentralization across different blockchain networks.

With Chainspect's Nakamoto Coefficient Rankings, you can compare the decentralization of leading blockchains, including Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, MultiversX, Polkadot, Monad, and many others. Track historical trends, compare validator and miner distributions, and identify which blockchain networks are the most decentralized.

Unlike simple validator or miner counts, the Nakamoto Coefficient measures how decentralized a network's control actually is.
