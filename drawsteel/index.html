Rolls: the important distinction

Power roll

The general 2d10 roll. Add the stated characteristic and modifiers, then find the tier: ≤11, 12–16, or 17+.

Ability roll

A power roll made for an ability, usually an attack or special power. Even tier 1 normally does something useful; the tier determines its impact.

Test

A power roll made to overcome an uncertain task: climb, sneak, persuade, investigate, and so on. The Director sets the characteristic and difficulty.

Saving throw

A separate 1d10 roll, normally made at the end of your turn against an effect marked (save ends). 6–10 succeeds; 1–5 fails.

Therefore: “+1 bonus to saving throws” changes only the d10 saving throw. It does not modify power rolls, ability rolls, or tests. A +1 saving-throw bonus makes an unmodified roll of 5 succeed because its total becomes 6.

Edges and banes apply to power rolls. One edge is +2; one bane is −2. A double edge raises the result by one tier; a double bane lowers it by one tier. Saving throws use bonuses only when a rule explicitly grants one.

The five characteristics

MMight

Strength, toughness, physical power.

AAgility

Speed, coordination, reflexes, precision.

RReason

Logic, memory, analysis, learned knowledge.

IIntuition

Awareness, instinct, empathy, reading situations.

PPresence

Confidence, force of personality, influence.

Your normal turn

1 main action

Usually a signature or heroic ability, a free strike, Charge, Defend, Heal, or another main action.

1 maneuver

For example Catch Breath, Aid Attack, Grab, Knockback, or a class ability labelled Maneuver.

1 move action

Usually Advance or Disengage. Movement can normally be divided around your other actions.

A free strike is a basic attack, not an automatically additional attack. A signature ability is an at-will special ability and usually takes your main action. Another effect can explicitly grant an extra free strike or signature ability.

Potency

Potency determines whether a target resists an additional effect. Read M < 2, bleeding as: “If the target’s Might is lower than 2, they become bleeding.” There is no extra roll.

Target MightM < 2Result
0 or 1TrueEffect applies
2 or moreFalseTarget resists

Spend 2 surges to increase potency by 1 against one target: M < 2 becomes M < 3. Potency can’t be increased by more than 1 using surges.

Metacurrencies and expendable resources

Heroic resources

Each class has its own currency—such as essence, wrath, or drama—which pays for heroic abilities. In combat you normally begin with an amount equal to your Victories, gain more each turn and from class triggers, and lose it when the encounter ends.

Surges

Temporary personal boosts. Spend a surge when dealing rolled damage to add damage equal to your highest characteristic (maximum 3 surges on one instance), or spend 2 to increase an ability’s potency by 1 against one target. Unspent surges disappear after combat.

Victories

The group earns Victories by overcoming encounters. They measure momentum between respites and determine how much heroic resource you have when combat begins. A respite converts accumulated Victories into advancement progress and resets them.

Hero tokens

A shared hero resource used to help the group when things go badly. The party gains them from rules such as a hero obtaining a tier-1 result on a test, and spends them on the options listed in the Hero Tokens rule.

Malice

The Director’s encounter currency, not a hero resource. The Director gains and spends it to power monster abilities and encounter effects. This is why some class features react when a Malice ability is used.

Recoveries

Your limited reserve of healing, rather than a heroic resource. Spending one normally restores your Recovery Value. Catch Breath and many healing abilities allow a creature to spend a Recovery.

Rules reference: Stamina & Recoveries

Stamina maximum

Your normal upper limit. Damage lowers current Stamina; healing cannot raise it above the maximum. Some effects can temporarily reduce the maximum itself.

Recovery Value

One-third of maximum Stamina, rounded down. Spending one Recovery normally restores this much Stamina. The number of Recoveries you have comes mainly from your class.

Winded Value

Half of maximum Stamina. At or below that value, you are winded. Winded has no automatic penalty by itself, but many abilities become stronger or weaker against a winded creature.

Death threshold

You die when your Stamina reaches the negative of your winded value. There are no death saving throws. Ordinary healing cannot revive a dead hero.

When can I spend a Recovery?

SituationHow it works
In combat, not dyingUse the Catch Breath maneuver: spend 1 Recovery and regain Stamina equal to your Recovery Value.
In combat, dyingYou cannot use Catch Breath yourself. An ally’s ability or another effect can still let you spend a Recovery. You remain able to act while dying.
Outside combatSpend as many remaining Recoveries as you wish, without using an action—even while dying.
Special abilitiesSome abilities let you or an ally spend a Recovery, add extra healing, permit multiple Recoveries, or restore Stamina without spending one. Follow the ability’s text.
After a respiteA completed 24-hour respite restores all Stamina and all spent Recoveries. Normal overnight sleep is not automatically a respite.

The Stamina states

Current StaminaStateWhat it means
Above winded valueHealthyNo special Stamina state.
Winded value or lower, but above 0WindedNo inherent penalty. Everyone can recognize that a creature is winded, and abilities can interact with this state.
0 or lower, but above the death thresholdDyingYou can still act, but cannot Catch Breath. You are also bleeding, and this dying-caused bleeding cannot be removed until you stop dying.
Negative winded value or lowerDeadOnly a special powerful effect or item can bring you back.
Example—maximum Stamina 18: Recovery Value is 6; winded value is 9. At 9 through 1 Stamina you are winded. At 0 through −8 you are dying. At −9 you die. If you are at −5 and an ally lets you spend a Recovery, you regain 6 Stamina and rise to 1—so you are no longer dying, though you remain winded.

Temporary Stamina

  • Damage removes temporary Stamina before normal Stamina.
  • Temporary Stamina does not change your maximum, Recovery Value, winded value, or whether you are winded, dying, or dead.
  • Multiple grants do not add together: keep the higher amount.
  • Regaining ordinary Stamina does not restore temporary Stamina.

Quick memory: one-third heals you; one-half means winded; zero means dying; negative one-half means dead.
